\begin{frame}{Step 1: Generating the watermark}
    The first step is to generate a uniformly random key-sequence $\xi \sim \mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]^{n \times |\mathcal{V}|}$ using our Mersenne Twister seeded by \texttt{key}.  We then use $\xi$ inside the decoder $\mathrm{Decode}(p,\xi_i)$ to sample tokens.

    Two popular decoding strategies are:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Inverse Transform Sampling (ITS)}: for each token, draw $u\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$ and pick the smallest token $t$ such that $\sum_{j\le t}p(j)\ge u$.  ITS exactly recovers the model distribution but requires sorting or cumulative sums.
        \item \textbf{Exp–Min Trick (EXP)}: for each vocabulary token $v$, compute independent exponential random variables $E_v=-\log\,\xi_v/p(v)$.  Then select $\arg\min_v E_v$.  One can show this also yields $t\sim p$, and it avoids cumulative sums by a simple \emph{min} operation over exponentials.  Moreover, EXP readily extends to dynamic programming for edit-robust detection.
    \end{itemize}

    In our example, we use the \emph{Exp–Min} method due to its implementation simplicity and natural extension to Levenshtein-based costs.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Step 3: Checking the watermark}
    To detect the watermark, we compute an \emph{alignment cost} between the candidate text $T$ and each cyclic shift of the key-sequence $\xi$.  The simplest cost is
    \[
        \mathrm{expCost}(T,\xi,\tau) = \sum_{i=1}^{|T|} -\log\bigl(1 - \xi_{(i+\tau)\bmod n}[T_i]\bigr),
    \]
    which is low when tokens follow the EXP selection uses.  For higher robustness, we use a
    \emph{Levenshtein-based cost}:
    \[
        \mathrm{expLevCost}(T,\xi,\tau) = \min_{\pi\in\mathrm{align}} \sum_{(i,j)\in\pi} c(i,j),
    \]
    where each substitution carries cost $-\log(1-\xi_{(j+\tau)\bmod n}[T_i])$, and each insertion or deletion costs a fixed $\gamma$

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Step 3: Checking the watermark}
    We then take the minimum cost over all offsets $\tau$, derive a p-value by permutation testing.

    To reiterate the p-values we obtained, we have:

        \begin{tabular}{ccc}
            \textbf{Plain/Control} & \textbf{Watermarked} & \textbf{Edited} \\
            \hline
            p=0.7228               & p=0.0099             & p=0.0099
        \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

    \bigskip

    A text is considered watermarked if $p<0.01$.
\end{frame}
